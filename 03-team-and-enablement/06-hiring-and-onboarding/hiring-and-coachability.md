# Hiring for Coachability and Curiosity, and How I Use Feedback

> **Done** means I did it as an SDR manager at an anonymized FP&A SaaS company or as a sales development leader at an anonymized enterprise procurement SaaS company. **Would do** means a step I would add or a situation I haven't faced. Outcomes are self-reported.

## What I've done (FP&A SaaS) — Done
- Grew the team from 7 to 12 SDRs with five internal hires.
- Four came from a hiring event in mid-December 2024, run like speed dating, with peer managers interviewing alongside me. I screened three of those four; my VP of Sales Development and a peer manager screened the fourth and recommended them highly.
- The fifth was a backfill already in process. I screened him and made the offer at the same time as the other four.

## Interview loop — Done
1. Recruiter screen
2. My screen (curiosity)
3. Case study presentation with a live role-play (coachability)
4. VP screen and offer

## Screening for curiosity — Done
I changed my questions for almost every screen. Three I used:
- "What did you look into about our company before today, and what did you want to know that you couldn't find?"
- "Tell me about a time you found a problem or opportunity others missed."
- "What questions do you have for me?" Scored on how specific the questions were.

## Testing coachability: the two-attempt role-play — Done
Run inside the case study presentation:
1. The candidate runs the role-play once.
2. They rate themselves first, before I say anything.
3. I give one specific note, not a list.
4. They run it again. Attempt 2 is scored on whether they used the note, not on overall polish.
5. I watch how they take the note: a clarifying question is a good sign, defensiveness is not.

Candidates who didn't apply the note didn't move forward.

## Hiring scorecard — Would do
A proposal, not something I've run: I'd turn the loop above into a written 1–5 scorecard, so every interviewer scores the same evidence and I compare scores before the VP screen.

| Trait | Where it's scored | Who scores | 1 looks like | 3 looks like | 5 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Curiosity | Hiring-manager screen | Me | Generic questions, no research done | Some research, questions stay surface-level | Specific questions built on their own research, plus an example of finding something others missed |
| Self-assessment | Role-play self-rating, before any feedback | Me | "Nothing to improve" | Names a minor gap | Names the same gap I saw, unprompted |
| Coachability | Role-play, attempt 2 | Me | Repeats attempt 1 or argues with the note | Applies the note partially | Applies the note and asks a clarifying question |
| Phone fit | Role-play, both attempts | Me and a peer manager | Can't hold the call structure | Holds the structure with prompting | Opens clearly, handles a pushback and asks for the meeting |

**Rules I'd add**
- Attempt 2 is scored on the change from attempt 1, not on polish.
- Scores are written down before the debrief, so no one is anchored by the first opinion.
- A 1 on coachability is a no regardless of the other scores; I'd review the rest case by case.
- After 90 days I'd compare scores with ramp results to check whether the scorecard predicts anything.

## Ramp and outcomes — Done
- All five were prospecting and on the phones by day 3. Daily activity started at 10 prospects and 20 dials and stepped up each day to 30 prospects and 150 dials by the end of week 2.
- All five were producing at least one meeting a week after two weeks.
- Where they went: one is an account manager at the same company, one became an AE, one became an SDR manager at a competitor, one is an enablement coordinator, and one joined me at my next company.

My weekly 1:1 format (one call reviewed, one priority, one commitment) and the call scorecard are in [coaching](../05-coaching/README.md), with a [worked example](../05-coaching/worked-scorecard-example.md).

## How I use critical feedback — Done

**How I ask for it**
- In 1:1s I ask reps what I should start, stop or keep doing as their manager, and I pressure-test it constantly.
- I ask my own manager for one thing to improve, at least every two weeks.
- I ask AEs for feedback on meeting quality and handoffs.

**Story 1: "You waited too long on underperformance" (VP of Global Sales Development, FP&A SaaS)**
- **Change:** I moved to earlier, written plans with daily and weekly numbers, and accountability check-ins at the end of every week, plus midweek whenever I noticed a rep slipping. Plans went over Slack, or by email when the VP or Director asked to be copied.
- **Result:** I raised misses two to three weeks in instead of at month or quarter end, and there were fewer surprise misses at period end. One rep on a 30-day plan (200+ dials and 30+ new prospects a day, 2 meetings a week, 3 SQLs by day 30) hit it, was promoted to Senior SDR a quarter later, and became a peer onboarding buddy for new SDRs.
- **Would do:** if a rep doesn't meet a plan, the written plan makes the next decision clear and fair. I haven't had to make that call.

**Story 2: "Show revenue impact, not activity or tool output" (enterprise procurement SaaS)**
- **Change:** I reported before-and-after numbers for every build.
- **Result:** I started tracking baselines, which is where the [Results page](../../05-proof-and-tools/results.md) numbers come from, and leadership reviews moved to pipeline and win rate.
